Visa & Duration

How Long Are You Allowed to Study English in the USA?

In the US market, how long you want to stay and how long you are allowed to stay are the same decision. The route you enter on sets your maximum study duration:

Entry routes

How Long Each Route Lets You Stay

US entry route and eligible course by length of stay
Length of stayVisa routeCourse
Up to 12 weeksESTA (Visa Waiver — eligible nationalities)GE20, or GE23 for more lessons per week
Typically up to 6 monthsB1/B2 visitor visaGE20 or GE23
Longer stays / 24+ lessons per weekF-1 student visaGE24 or GE28

You can study English in the USA for up to 12 weeks on ESTA (if eligible); courses of 24 or more lessons per week require an F-1 visa. A B1/B2 visitor visa typically allows English study for up to 6 months.

CEL’s General English 23 (23.75 lessons per week) stays below the 24-lesson F-1 threshold — more lessons per week, still on ESTA.

CEL provides course guidance, not visa advice — eligibility and duration are decided by U.S. authorities. travel.state.gov

What Affects Progress

What Affects How Fast You Learn?

Your starting level

Higher levels take slightly longer: refining complex arguments at C1 is slower work than learning everyday phrases at A1. The ladder above shows the typical range — approx. 8 to 11 weeks per level.

Course intensity

More weekly lessons compress calendar time. CEL offers GE20 (20 lessons/week), GE23 (23.75 lessons/week — more lessons, still on ESTA), GE24 (24 lessons/week, F-1) and GE28 (28 lessons/week, F-1). Compare weekly prices on the cost page.

Daily immersion

In Pacific Beach, class is only part of your English day — you order, ask, negotiate and joke in English in shops, at the beach and in your shared apartment or homestay. Students who use English outside class progress faster.

Personal motivation

Attendance, homework and the willingness to speak from day one make measurable differences — they’re the variables you control.

Courses & Electives

How Do You Structure a Longer Stay?

On a longer stay you don’t repeat the same course — you build a pathway. CEL San Diego’s elective areas are Career Development Skills, Academic Skills, TOEFL Exam Preparation and Cambridge Exam Preparation.

TOEFL and Cambridge exam electives run for up to 8 weeks; most exam students start with General English and switch to exam preparation later in their stay. Cambridge is more mixed: higher-level candidates sometimes book only the 8-week Cambridge block, others start with General English and switch.

Same intensity, same price.

Courses of the same intensity cost the same — you can switch from General English to TOEFL preparation or Career Development mid-stay at no extra cost.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How many weeks do I need to improve my English?

+

Approx. 10 weeks per CEFR level; even 4 weeks brings noticeable speaking gains. Where you start and how many lessons per week you take set your exact pace.

How long can I study English in the USA without a student visa?

+

Up to 12 weeks on ESTA if eligible; a B1/B2 visitor visa typically allows up to 6 months. Courses of 24+ lessons per week always require an F-1 visa. See the visa table and our F-1 visa page.

Is 12 weeks enough to see real progress?

+

Yes — for most students, 12 weeks is enough to complete one full CEFR level, which is when progress becomes clearly noticeable. It is also the most popular course length at CEL San Diego.

Can I extend my course once I’m in San Diego?

+

Extending your course is easy in-school — but whether you can extend your stay depends on how you entered the USA. An ESTA stay cannot be extended beyond 90 days — students planning more than 12 weeks need a B1/B2 visitor visa (typically up to 6 months) or an F-1 visa. Plan your intended duration before you enter the USA.

Can I study for only 2–4 weeks?

+

Yes — courses start any Monday from 1 week; expect fluency and confidence gains, not a full level.

How long does it take from B1 to B2?

+

From B1 to B2 takes about 10 weeks of full-time study on average.

Does studying longer reduce the weekly price?

+

Yes — weekly tuition drops at 7, 13 and 20 weeks. See the cost page.

How long should I prepare for TOEFL or Cambridge?

+

The exam electives run for up to 8 weeks. Most TOEFL students start with General English and switch to exam preparation later in their stay, so total stays are usually longer than 8 weeks. Higher-level Cambridge candidates sometimes book only the 8-week exam block.

Do beginners learn faster than advanced students?

+

In weeks per level, yes — slightly. Early levels cover high-frequency language you use every day, so progress feels fast (approx. 8–9 weeks for A1–A2). Moving from upper-intermediate to advanced takes longer (approx. 10–11 weeks for B2–C1) because you are refining complex arguments, precision and range rather than adding basics. That’s normal — it’s the plateau every learner meets, and structured classes are what push you through it.

How does your living situation affect progress?

+

Students who live in English progress faster. In a homestay you negotiate daily life with your hosts in English; in a shared student apartment you live with other internationals whose only common language is English. Both extend immersion beyond the classroom — choose by lifestyle, not by fear of speaking. See housing options.

How does an academic-year stay work?

+

An academic year is roughly 35–40 weeks — enough to go from beginner to B2, or from intermediate level through C1 with time for exam preparation. Stays of this length run on an F-1 student visa with GE24 or GE28, and university-bound students can continue into the Pathway Program USA. Weekly tuition drops at 7, 13 and 20 weeks, so academic-year stays also have the lowest weekly rates. See the F-1 visa page.

How long does it take to become fluent in English?

+

There is no single definition of fluency. Many students consider B2 fluent for work, travel and daily life, while C1 is typically needed for university study or professional writing. From beginner to B2 takes roughly 35–40 weeks — about one academic year. Reaching C1 from beginner takes approximately 48 weeks — the sum of CEL’s per-level averages.

San Diego or Vancouver for a longer stay?

+

Both CEL cities teach the same methodology with the same approx. 10-weeks-per-level average — the difference is the setting and the paperwork. San Diego offers up to 12 visa-free weeks on ESTA (eligible nationalities; B1/B2 visitor visa otherwise) and TOEFL preparation for US universities; Vancouver suits students targeting Canada.
